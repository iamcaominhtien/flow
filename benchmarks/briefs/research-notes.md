This is what the tool that wrote the briefs said about its own work, copied as it was, with the citation numbers it left glued to words. It is **not verified**; [CHECKS.md](CHECKS.md) has what was measured here.

## Problems (self-reported)

During test suite compilation and graph validation, all twenty-four briefs satisfied the quantitative cardinality bounds, structural complexity thresholds, and syntax constraints1. No diagram brief required pruning or failed to reach its specified target complexity tier:

* Difficulty Tier Ingestion: Level A briefs consistently maintain 8 to 12 elements with strictly linear or single-decision branching topographies. Level B briefs incorporate between 20 and 24 elements with multiple grouped boundaries and at least two functional feedback loops. Level C briefs contain between 42 and 46 elements (comfortably centered within the 40–60 element target range) and between 44 and 56 connections, successfully executing multi-tier transitive edge skips, 3+ degree hub fan-outs, cross-group edges, and nested recovery cycles.  
* Graph-Theoretic Endpoint Audit: Across all twenty-four briefs, every connection explicitly names both origin and destination vertices within the brief text, preventing unresolved edge references or ambiguous dangling endpoints in declarative parsing compilers.  
* Domain Disambiguation: Twenty-four distinct real-world systems were synthesized without thematic reuse. All briefs avoid unsupported visual features such as vendor proprietary logos, calendar-bound dates, mobile hardware device wrappers, and BPMN data objects or boundary interrupting events.
